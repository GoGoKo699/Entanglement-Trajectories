# Frequently Asked Questions

## Why are there so many entanglement measures?

A Schmidt spectrum contains many degrees of freedom. A scalar measure compresses them with a particular sensitivity. Different measures coexist because they answer different questions about the leading edge, bulk, and tail.

## What unifies them here?

For a fixed cut of a bipartite pure state, they are functions of one ordered Schmidt spectrum. Its time evolution is the common path; the metric trajectories are projections of that path.

## Does unification mean that all metrics agree?

No. Majorization defines when Schur-concave metrics must agree. Majorization-incomparable spectra can be ordered differently by different Rényi sectors. The atlas unifies the source of both consensus and competition.

## Is pure-state logarithmic negativity an independent family?

It is exactly the Rényi-$1/2$ entropy for a bipartite pure state. It is independent of the order-1 and order-2 classes in the sense relevant to spectral sensitivity, but it should not be counted separately from Rényi-$1/2$ under another name.

## Is linear entropy independent of purity and Rényi-2 entropy?

No. They are strict transformations of the same spectral moment $\sum_i\lambda_i^2$. The registry groups them into one equivalence class.

## Has a topological invariant been proved?

No. The quoted phrase refers to a coarse metric-robust trajectory class. Fine path features, including exact turn counts, are not invariant. Formal topology is an open direction.

## Why use $\lambda_{\max}$ as a horizontal coordinate?

It captures the spectral head and is equivalent to min-entropy and standard bipartite geometric coordinates. It also conditions exact feasible envelopes for the vertical metrics. It is informative but not a complete description of the spectrum.

## Does random matrix theory define the boundary?

No. Majorization gives the exact finite-dimensional feasible boundary. Haar/Wishart, Marchenko–Pastur, and spiked-Wishart constructions are conditional reference ensembles inside that arena.

## Do all tested systems approach a random-matrix attractor?

No. Some selected trajectories approach some reference diagnostics closely, some rebound, and others remain far away. Different scalar, density, edge, and correlation diagnostics can disagree.

## Does a quantum Fourier transform preserve entanglement?

Not in general. The published paper’s overlap was special to selected state families. The correction package includes an explicit two-qubit counterexample.

## Can the trajectories identify the underlying dynamics?

They carry model information at the centroid level. Generalization to unseen individual conditions is weaker and remains preliminary. The strictest current test does not beat the $\lambda_{\max}$-path baseline.

## Does low entropy imply efficient classical simulation?

Not by itself. Efficient tensor-network approximation also depends on Schmidt-tail control, the relevant cuts, and the demanded accuracy. The repository removes the paper’s overly broad computational interpretation.

## Does high entropy imply computational usefulness or uselessness?

Neither conclusion follows from entropy magnitude alone. Operational resource value and computational hardness require separate analyses.

## Does this cover mixed-state or multipartite entanglement?

No. The main exact and empirical claim is fixed-cut bipartite pure-state dynamics. Aggregates over several cuts are labeled explicitly and are not silently promoted to genuine multipartite invariants.

## Is this a second paper?

The follow-up work is presented as a corrected and extended research companion to the published paper, not as a separate journal claim. Its purpose is to make the original philosophy precise, reproducible, and properly bounded.
