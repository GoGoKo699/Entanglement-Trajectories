# Operational meaning of the quoted “topological invariant”

## Philosophy

The phrase “topological invariant” describes a visual observation: when the entanglement metric changes, the path can remain recognizably associated with the same underlying dynamics. Its operational meaning here is tested through empirical trajectory comparisons.

The primary object is the Schmidt-spectrum path

\[
\Gamma:t\mapsto\boldsymbol\lambda(t).
\]

Each metric class supplies a projection

\[
P_E\Gamma(t)=\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(t),r_E(t)\bigr),
\]

where \(r_E\) is the exact-boundary-relative metric coordinate. The family \(\{P_E\Gamma\}\) is the entanglement-trajectory atlas.

## Operational invariant

The supported invariant is a **coarse equivalence class of projections** characterized by several jointly reported properties:

1. a dominant common metric mode;
2. stable temporal ordering across metric coordinates;
3. partial preservation of distances and nearest-neighbor relations among trajectories;
4. model-centroid paths that remain distinguishable across metrics and sizes;
5. stable coarse descriptors such as arc-length ordering;
6. explicit localization of metric contradictions to majorization-incomparable full-spectrum steps in the selected audit.

No single score defines the class. The evidence is a structured profile, not a binary theorem.

## What is not invariant

The data directly reject stronger interpretations:

- numerical coordinates are not equal;
- the order-2 class can deviate substantially from the order-1 and order-1/2 classes;
- exact vertical-turn counts usually change;
- signed areas are only moderately preserved;
- an individual unseen trajectory is not reliably identified under the strictest holdout;
- self-intersections, curvature, and local extrema are not guaranteed to survive projection.

These comparisons establish an empirical morphology, rather than a formal topological invariant.

## Preferred terminology

**Metric-robust trajectory class** and **projection-stable trajectory morphology** name the empirical object. **Common metric mode** describes shared variation, while **metric-consensus and metric-competitive motion** distinguish local responses. The quoted phrase **“topological invariant”** carries this operational qualification.
